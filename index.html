  1. Apr 2026 — Present

    Software Engineer I

    Redo · Series B, high-growth e‑commerce startup · Draper, Utah

    I work on international returns, the part of Redo that lets a shopper in London or Sydney send a product back to a merchant in another country. I've been one of two engineers on it, and the only one for four months, while the business grew 2.5x. Much of that growth runs on lanes and features I built.

    Read moreShow less

    Pricing that pays for itself

    International labels used to be passed through near carrier cost. I designed a market-rate pricing model, with rate cards by package weight and destination country, that now captures $250K+ a year in margin Redo was leaving on the table.

    Getting returns through customs

    Half of our international returns were getting held at the border. I built one system that prepares every customs declaration: real HS codes, honest countries of origin, importer and shopper tax IDs, and duties-paid terms. Every carrier now receives the same correct paperwork. Customs delays fell from 50% to 10%.

    Opening new lanes

    I integrated 10+ carrier APIs across the UK, EU, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand. That ranged from European parcel networks to carrier-collected pickups for bulky items, each with its own address rules, label formats, and failure modes.

    Labels that fix themselves

    When a carrier rejects a label, an LLM agent proposes a corrected address, a deterministic safety gate checks the change against reference data, and the label is bought on retry. That has recovered 200+ failed labels. A second agent studies the failures it can't fix, traces them to a root cause, and opens a code fix for review: 25 so far.

    A fleet of agents

    Beyond labels, I run autonomous agents that triage production alerts, investigate root causes, and implement customs features. They do the legwork and hand me well-tested merge requests. Every change still gets human review.
